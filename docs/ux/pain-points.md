# UX Pain Points: Albion Fan Hub

## Project Context

Albion Fan Hub is an unofficial, self-directed UX/UI case study exploring how a football club could make matchday information easier to access.

The pain points below are initial hypotheses based on the project scenario and persona. They have not yet been validated through formal user research.

## 1. Slow Performance

**User problem:** Important pages or information may take too long to load when a supporter needs them quickly.

**Impact:** The supporter may become frustrated, waste time or struggle to access information while travelling or at the stadium.

**Design opportunity:** Create a lightweight, mobile-first interface and prioritise essential information.

**Priority:** High

## 2. Delayed or Unclear Updates

**User problem:** Matchday information may not update quickly enough, or the supporter may not know how recently it was updated.

**Impact:** The supporter may be unsure whether information is current and reliable.

**Design opportunity:** Display clear update timestamps and distinguish important announcements from general content. Any live-data behaviour must be supported by a reliable data source.

**Priority:** High

## 3. Difficult Navigation

**User problem:** The supporter may need to visit multiple pages or navigate several menus to find the information they want.

**Impact:** Finding essential information takes longer than necessary, particularly on a phone.

**Design opportunity:** Create a clear matchday dashboard with prominent shortcuts to the most useful information.

**Priority:** High

## Initial MVP Direction

The first version should focus on:

- A clear, mobile-friendly matchday dashboard.
- Important match information displayed prominently.
- Simple navigation to travel, stadium and ticket information.
- Visible timestamps for updates where appropriate.
- Accessible controls, readable text and clear visual hierarchy.

## How We'll Evaluate the Design

We can test the prototype with realistic tasks, such as finding kick-off information or locating stadium travel details.

Possible measures include:

- Time taken to find key information.
- Whether users complete each task successfully.
- Number of unnecessary navigation steps.
- Feedback on clarity and ease of use.

These are proposed evaluation methods, not results from completed user testing.
